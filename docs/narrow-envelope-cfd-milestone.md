# Narrow-Envelope CFD Milestone and Acceleration Policy

## Status

This change establishes a reproducible first verification gate and a controlled
performance path. It does not claim that FlowLab now has a validated CFD
solution, a CAD-quality production mesh, or a trained learned surrogate.

The authoritative result class remains an OpenFOAM result with captured solver
evidence. A benchmark or an accelerator output cannot promote itself to that
class.

The captured `2026-07-13-ogrid-v2` package passes its narrow serial gates
(0.2504239% apparent pressure-drop error, zero mass imbalance, and 0.2832664%
fine-grid GCI) and its exact-input 2-rank/4-rank QoI gate (largest difference
3.0459997e-10). Its independent review retains it as analysis-only: its fixed
64-facet inscribed polygon has a 0.1605607% area deficit, and its two-direction
GCI does not bound wall-facet or core resolution error. See
`benchmarks/cases/straight-pipe/runs/2026-07-13-ogrid-v2/artifacts/internal-technical-review.md`.

## First Reference Problem

The first problem is a steady, single-phase, incompressible Newtonian,
fully-developed laminar flow through a straight circular pipe.

- The analytical reference is Hagen-Poiseuille pressure drop.
- The reference-bundle generator rejects Reynolds numbers at or above 2100. An
  execution must impose a fully developed inlet profile or retain an explicit
  entrance-length justification before comparing pressure drop to the reference.
- The required comparison QoIs are pressure drop in Pa and mass flow rate in
  kg/s.
- The current first-milestone validator requires exactly three uniformly
  refined, coarse-to-fine mesh levels with a ratio of at least 1.3, then
  recomputes the observed order and fine-grid Grid Convergence Index. The
  predeclared fine-grid GCI limit is 1.0%.
- Time refinement is required for transient or pseudo-transient runs; a direct
  steady solve needs explicit evidence for why it is inapplicable.
- The predeclared candidate gates are a pressure-drop relative error no greater
  than 0.05 and a relative inlet/outlet mass-flow imbalance no greater than
  0.001.
- All gates require case-manifest, mesh, solver, raw-field, residual-history,
  mesh-quality, and canonical QoI-extraction evidence before independent
  review.
- The local metric helper is analysis-only: even if caller-provided values meet
  the thresholds, it cannot emit a passed or validated benchmark result.

The concrete pending contract is in
benchmarks/cases/straight-pipe/benchmark.json. The deterministic validator is
server/flowlab/benchmark_fixtures.py. It rejects observed numerical values while
the fixture is pending, so the repository cannot turn a planned result into
evidence by typing numbers into the fixture.

server/flowlab/verification.py adds an SI-unit 3D Gmsh/OpenCASCADE circular-pipe
template plus analytical and Richardson/GCI helpers. Template creation is
explicitly unverified: it is not a substitute for running Gmsh, converting a
mesh, running OpenFOAM, or reviewing results.

`server/flowlab/straight_pipe_runner.py` is the executable counterpart for the
narrow pipe envelope. New runs use a five-block, full-pipe O-grid with periodic
axial boundaries and no collapsed axis. Its wall is still a fixed 64-facet
inscribed polygon generated by `blockMesh`, not a CAD-exact curved surface; the
fixed wall geometry, central diamond core, and their area deficit are retained
in each case manifest and QoI record. The three-grid sequence refines axial and
annular-radial spacing while keeping those resolved core/wall pieces fixed, so
its GCI is explicitly a fixed-geometry, two-direction refinement result—not a
claim of a complete geometry/mesh continuum limit. The legacy 90-degree sector
recipe remains readable for historical v3 evidence but is not reinterpreted as
the new O-grid family.

The runner uses OpenFOAM v11 `meanVelocityForce` to impose the declared
volumetric flow rate over the actual faceted mesh area and extracts pressure
drop from the converged kinematic pressure-gradient source. It retains native
fields, VTK, checkMesh reports, logs, runtime records, a QoI table, and
hash-indexed serial evidence. Runtime records bind the runner-source hash and
Docker image ID/repository digest when Docker can expose them. Periodic pressure
has a gauge null space, so the runner requires predeclared controller-tail
stability, continuity, and inner linear-solver criteria rather than treating a
normalized zero-pressure residual as physical non-convergence.

New materializations also archive the runner source bytes as
`runner-source-snapshot.py` and bind its SHA-256 in each case manifest. This
prevents a source edit made during a long solve from being mistaken for the
source that created the case. Existing indexed captures are not rewritten to
backfill that file.

For a captured promotion, the QoI table is a hash-verified JSON artifact. Its
SI reference inputs, three raw-result-linked pressure-drop samples, and signed
mass flows are the source of truth. The validator derives Reynolds number,
Hagen-Poiseuille pressure drop, analytical error, mass imbalance, observed
order, and GCI from it; a matching-looking summary cannot satisfy the gate by
itself. SHA-256 binds the package internally, but independent-review identity
and solver origin still require a controlled human/release process rather than
being cryptographically proven by this repository.

This follows NASA's core CFD verification practices: iterative convergence,
conservation, spatial and temporal convergence, and comparison to an analytical
or benchmark reference. See [NASA CFD Verification Assessment](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/verassess.html).

## Measured Performance Path

Parallel execution is an opt-in OpenFOAM candidate for only the current narrow
incompressible Navier-Stokes starter mode. It is not enabled for heat transfer,
multiphase, CHT, cavitation, or transient modes, because those modes do not yet
have separate parallel verification evidence.

A project may opt in with:

    {
      "solver": {
        "performance": {
          "openfoamParallel": {
            "enabled": true,
            "ranks": 4,
            "decomposition": "scotch"
          }
        }
      }
    }

The generated case then contains:

- system/decomposeParDict with the requested rank count and scotch method;
- constant/flowlab_openfoam_parallel_plan.json, which records that a serial
  baseline and benchmark measurements are required;
- an Allrun sequence that performs mesh generation or uses the fitted mesh,
  runs serial and decomposed parallel checkMesh, launches foamRun under MPI,
  reconstructs the latest result, and then exports VTK if available.

The generated-case validator recognizes only direct, uncommented command lines
and checks that the sequence is serial `checkMesh` → `decomposePar` → parallel
`checkMesh` → MPI `foamRun` → `reconstructPar`. It does not treat an `echo`, a
comment, or an out-of-order command as execution evidence.

OpenFOAM documents this decomposition/MPI/reconstruction workflow. See the
[OpenFOAM v11 parallel example](https://doc.cfd.direct/openfoam/user-guide-v11/dambreak)
and [parallel decomposition reference](https://doc.openfoam.com/2312/tools/parallel/).

The parallel plan intentionally has no speedup number. The first runtime
experiment must keep the mesh, numerics, stopping criteria, and QoI extraction
identical and record wall time, CPU time, peak memory, cells per rank, iteration
count, runtime image digest, conservation, and the straight-pipe QoIs for
serial, 2-rank, 4-rank, and then higher-rank runs. Choose the smallest
configuration that meets the target latency; low-cell-count cases commonly lose
time to decomposition, MPI, and reconstruction overhead.

The straight-pipe runner enforces this order for its first MPI evidence: a
serial-gate-passed candidate must select `fine`; then only its `0/`, `constant/`,
`system/`, and case manifest are copied into isolated 2-rank and 4-rank cases.
The inputs are hashed before adding `decomposeParDict`, and the harness runs
serial checkMesh, decomposition, parallel checkMesh, MPI solve, reconstruction,
and VTK export. It compares pressure drop, pressure gradient, full-pipe flow,
and signed inlet/outlet mass flows to the serial QoIs at a predeclared 1e-6
relative tolerance. MPI logs, fields, QoI results, and their evidence package
are archived and hash-indexed separately from the serial gate artifacts.

The captured `periodic-v3` and `ogrid-v2` 1/2/4-rank values are valid local
exact-input QoI evidence, but not a portable speedup conclusion: Docker Desktop
ran an AMD64 image through an ARM64 engine and each rank count was a single
unpinned trial.
Before reporting a production-performance claim, repeat a fresh serial V&V
suite and then at least five measured serial/2-rank/4-rank trials on a native
matching image/engine architecture. Include one or more warm-ups, Docker
`--cpuset-cpus` masks, per-trial QoI gates, solver ClockTime and host wall time,
median/MAD/min/max summaries, image identity, and an explicit statement that a
Docker Desktop VM logical-CPU mask is not physical-core pinning.

The runner exposes that protocol as `--run-replicated-timing`, with explicit
`--serial-cpuset`, `--mpi2-cpuset`, and `--mpi4-cpuset` arguments. It writes a
native-preflight record before any solver work. The local 2026-07-13 record at
`benchmarks/cases/straight-pipe/runs/2026-07-13-native-timing-preflight-v1`
correctly rejected timing: its Docker Engine is Linux/ARM64 while the pinned
image is Linux/AMD64. It records the requested logical CPU masks but reports no
performance data and makes no CPU-pinning claim.

## Learned-Acceleration Boundary

DeepCFD and CFD-GCN are useful design references, but neither is a drop-in
replacement for an authoritative FlowLab result.

| Reference | What is useful | Why it is not enabled now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [DeepCFD](https://github.com/mdribeiro/DeepCFD) and its [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08826) | Narrow, fixed-family models can make field estimates extremely quickly once they have a carefully bounded dataset. | Its published setting is 2D steady laminar flow around synthetic obstacles with a raster/U-Net-style representation; that is not evidence for FlowLab's current 3D, mesh, or physics scope. |
| [CFD-GCN](https://github.com/locuslab/cfd-gcn) and its [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04439) | Coarse physical solve plus graph correction is a stronger long-term match for unstructured CFD meshes, warm starts, and correction models. | Its reported airfoil setting is still a bounded 2D inviscid benchmark; it does not verify FlowLab geometry, viscous wall treatment, or a user-selected operating envelope. |

server/flowlab/acceleration.py therefore implements only a policy gate, not a
model. A future surrogate or warm-start candidate must declare:

- an exact geometry family, physics tuple, QoI list, and Reynolds-number range;
- a validated benchmark set;
- solver version, container image digest, mesh recipe hash, and dataset manifest
  hash for the training data;
- an explicit output label of surrogate estimate or warm-start candidate.

No learned-model release is registered in this repository today, so every
surrogate request currently returns authoritative-cfd-required. A future
release must be backed by filesystem-verified validated benchmarks and a
separately reviewed immutable model/calibration/OOD artifact registry. Even an
in-envelope result is never labelled CFD-converged or validated-CFD and requires
an authoritative OpenFOAM spot check.

## Next Execution Gate

1. The captured `periodic-v3` and fixed-geometry `ogrid-v2` packages have been
   independently reviewed as analysis-only evidence; do not promote their
   emulated timing or treat the O-grid as CAD exact.
2. Run a controlled wall-facet/angular and core-resolution sensitivity at fixed
   spatial resolution. It must hold the physics, flow rate, convergence
   criteria, and QoIs constant, quantify geometry-induced pressure-drop change,
   then select an accepted geometry before a fresh three-grid GCI study.
   The attempted 128-facet family at the current coarse spacing is retained as
   a rejected `checkMesh` preflight, not a V&V result. The required CAD-cylinder
   frozen-surface contract and Gmsh/OpenFOAM preflight are in
   `docs/cad-cylinder-geometry-gate.md`.
   The current Gmsh 4.4 frozen-surface and CAD-solver-smoke probes are blocked
   rather than silently accepted; their retained failure evidence is at
   `benchmarks/cases/straight-pipe/runs/2026-07-13-cad-smoke-and-surface-blockers-v1`.
3. Provision a native matching OpenFOAM image/engine architecture, rerun the
   O-grid V&V there, and only then collect repeated CPU-set serial/2-rank/4-rank
   timing with QoI equivalence on every trial.
4. Expand one physics boundary at a time—developing flow, non-circular
   passages, then transient flow—with a separate analytical or experimental
   reference and independent mesh/GCI/QoI gate set.
5. Promote only when the independent review confirms all provenance,
   conservation, grid-convergence, geometry-sensitivity, and analytical-error
   gates for that declared envelope.
6. After at least several validated cases exist inside one tightly declared
   envelope, assess a QoI predictor or OpenFOAM warm-start model before any
   full-field neural surrogate.

The desired outcome is not generic benchmark speed. It is a small operating
envelope whose latency, uncertainty, and fallback behavior are all measured and
reviewable.
